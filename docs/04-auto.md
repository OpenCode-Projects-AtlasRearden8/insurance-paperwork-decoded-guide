# Auto Policies, Translated

> **Education only.** This guide explains what auto policy sections *are*.
> Your policy's wording and your state's rules control. Nothing here tells
> you what coverage to buy or carry.

An auto policy has a consistent skeleton across carriers: declarations,
definitions, insuring agreements split into **liability** and **damage to
your car**, exclusions, conditions, endorsements. Here's each piece in
plain English.

## The coverage rows you'll see

### Liability (bodily injury + property damage)

Pays *other people* when an at-fault accident is yours. Limits are written
as three numbers, e.g. `100/300/50` meaning (in thousands): $100k bodily
injury per person, $300k bodily injury per accident, $50k property damage
per accident. This is the coverage most states legally require. It never
pays for your own car or your own injuries.

### Collision

Pays for damage to *your* car from hitting something or being hit —
regardless of fault — minus your collision deductible. If your car is
financed or leased, your lender almost always requires it.

### Comprehensive ("other than collision")

Pays for damage to your car from things that aren't collisions: theft,
weather, fire, falling objects, animal strikes, glass. Has its own
deductible, often smaller.

### Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)

Pays *you* when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little. Some
states require it; the dec page shows whether you carry it.

### Medical payments (MedPay) or Personal Injury Protection (PIP)

Pays medical costs for you and your passengers after an accident, mostly
regardless of fault. PIP (in no-fault states) can also cover lost wages.
Limits are small and listed flatly on the dec page.

### Extras

Roadside assistance, rental reimbursement (a *daily* limit, e.g. $30/day,
and a *total* cap, e.g. $900), loan/lease gap coverage. Each row's limit
format is different — read the units.

## Reading the exclusions section

Auto exclusion headlines usually include: intentional acts, racing,
using the car as a taxi or for delivery (unless endorsed), vehicles not
listed on the policy, normal mechanical wear, and business use beyond what
the policy contemplates. Skim the headlines; deep-read only what matches
your life (if you ever drive for a delivery app, that headline is yours).

## The conditions that matter most

- **Duties after an accident:** report promptly, cooperate, don't admit
  fault, get a police report when required. Deadlines are here.
- **Subrogation:** if the other driver was at fault, your insurer may pay
  you first and recover from them later.
- **Cancellation and non-renewal:** both sides' rules, notice periods, and
  reasons.

## A claim-shaped mental model

Picture any accident, then map it to rows:

- Someone's hurt (not you) → liability, bodily injury.
- A mailbox is flattened → liability, property damage.
- Your bumper is crumpled, other driver fled → collision (or UM if it was a
  hit-and-run driver, depending on your state and policy).
- Hail dents the hood → comprehensive.
- You need a rental while it's in the shop → rental reimbursement, at $X/day
  up to $Y.

If you can map an event to a row, you can predict which deductible applies
and roughly what the money math will look like (guide 02's arithmetic).

## The annual auto check (30 seconds each)

During the guide-11 ritual, glance at: listed drivers (anyone moved out?),
listed vehicles (VINs right?), mileage estimates (a big driving change
matters), and the rental-reimbursement daily cap (shop rates went up
everywhere; caps from 2019 read quaint).
