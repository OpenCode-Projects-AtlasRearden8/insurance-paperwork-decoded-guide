# The Annual Review Ritual

> **Education only.** This ritual is about *reading and organizing your own
> paperwork*. It never recommends coverage changes — decisions belong to
> you and the licensed professionals you choose.

Once a year, insurance paperwork deserves one focused hour. Not a weekend,
not a filing system overhaul — one hour, once, anchored to a date you
can't forget. This guide is that hour, scripted.

## When to run it

Anchor it to something that already happens:

- **Renewal season:** when any policy's renewal packet arrives, run the
  ritual for that policy (guide 03's dec-page reading is half the ritual).
- **A fixed calendar day:** your birthday month, the new year, daylight
  saving time — any anchor works. One policy per month across the year
  also works: five policies, five light months.
- **A life event:** move, new job, new family member, paid-off car. Run
  the ritual early when life changes.

Pick one anchor and put it in your calendar now, with the note "insurance
hour — tea first."

## The hour, in ten small steps

Each step is small enough to finish on a low-energy day; if you only do
step 1 this year, that's still a real win.

1. **Gather.** One stack: every policy's dec page, the binder folder,
   this guide. (10 min including finding the stack.)
2. **Five numbers, per policy.** Premium, deductible(s), main limit(s),
   period end date, claim phone number → Worksheet A. (Guide 03.)
3. **People check.** Named insureds, listed drivers, beneficiaries,
   mortgagee/loss-payee lines. Anything stale gets a note: "call about
   ___."
4. **Life-drift check.** What changed this year? New job, moved, bought
   anything above a special-limit cap (jewelry, electronics, bikes),
   renovation, new driver in the house? Each change gets a note — not a
   decision, just a note.
5. **Endorsement skim.** Compare this year's forms list to last year's
   (Worksheet B keeps the list). New endorsements get their first
   paragraph read.
6. **Deadline refresh.** Claim-reporting deadlines and appeal deadlines →
   Worksheet A and E. (Guides 01, 07, 10.)
7. **Document sweep.** One folder per policy, newest dec page on top.
   Shred nothing you're unsure about; scan-or-photograph one copy of each
   dec page if you keep digital backups.
8. **Health specifics.** Network changes for your usual providers,
   prescription tier changes, EOB arithmetic spot-check from the year
   (one EOB, line by line, guide 07). Medicare households: the companion
   medicare-decoded-guide has its own ritual for the Annual Notice of
   Change.
9. **The note list.** Compile every "call about ___" note from steps 3–8
   onto one page. These are *questions*, not decisions — for your agent,
   carrier, or consumer office. One call session later in the week
   handles the list.
10. **Close the binder.** Date the inside cover. Done for the year. The
    folder goes back wherever it lives — one place, always the same
    place.

## The policy binder (physical or digital)

The binder is just five labeled sections, matching the worksheets:

- **A — Snapshots:** one Worksheet A per policy, most recent on top.
- **B — Policies:** the full policy documents and endorsement lists.
- **C — Claims:** one folder per claim, however small, with the
  call-log notes.
- **D — Inventories:** home inventory (Worksheet D) and receipts for big
  items.
- **E — Correspondence:** denial letters, appeal letters and their
  deadlines, EOBs you're still tracking.

A binder is finished when a stranger could find any policy's numbers in
under a minute. That's the only standard.

## What the ritual deliberately does not do

It doesn't compare prices, recommend products, or make coverage decisions.
It produces the *clean snapshot* that makes any future decision fast —
five numbers per policy and a list of questions. Decisions made from a
clean snapshot, with professional input, are the good kind.

## The low-energy fallback

Worst year ever? Do step 2 only: five numbers per policy, one worksheet
per policy, done. That single page answers 80% of what life will ask.
Everything else waits a year without harm.
