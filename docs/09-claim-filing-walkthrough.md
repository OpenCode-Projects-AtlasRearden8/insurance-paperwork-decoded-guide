# Filing a Claim: Walkthroughs and Checklists

> **Education only.** This guide describes how claim processes generally
> work so you can navigate them calmly. It is not instructions for any
> specific claim, and it never tells you whether to file.

A claim is a paperwork project with a deadline. Like any project, it has a
start kit, a middle, and an end. This guide gives you the general shape and
the checklists; your policy's conditions section (guide 01, part 5) gives
the deadlines that actually apply.

## The universal shape of a claim

1. **Safety and stabilization.** People first. Property second: policies'
   conditions typically require you to take reasonable steps to prevent
   further damage (a tarp on the roof, not a renovation).
2. **Report the claim.** Policy number, date/time, what happened, where,
   who's involved. Most carriers take first notice by phone or app. Note
   the claim number they give you — every later contact starts with it.
3. **Document before you clean up.** Photos and video before anything is
   moved or repaired, if safe to do so.
4. **The adjuster.** The insurer's investigator for your claim. They'll
   inspect, request documents, and produce an estimate or decision.
5. **The resolution.** Payment (minus deductible), a denial with stated
   reasons, or a request for more information. Guide 10 handles the denial
   case.

## Document checklist: master list

Copy this into your binder front page. Not every claim needs everything;
the checklist is one list so you never hunt twice.

**Always:**

- [ ] Policy number and dec page copy
- [ ] Claim number (assigned at first notice)
- [ ] Date, time, location, one-paragraph description of what happened
- [ ] Photos/video, timestamped if possible, taken *before* cleanup
- [ ] Police or incident report number, if one exists
- [ ] Names and contact info of anyone involved or witnessing

**For property claims (auto/home/renters):**

- [ ] Photos of damage from multiple angles plus one wide shot
- [ ] Inventory of damaged/lost items (Worksheet D): item, age, rough
      replacement cost, receipt if you have it
- [ ] Receipts for immediate needs (hotel, tarp, board-up) — loss-of-use
      and mitigation costs are receipt-driven
- [ ] Repair estimates (get more than one where practical)
- [ ] Any contractor or adjuster report you receive

**For health claims:**

- [ ] The EOB for each service (guide 07 decodes it)
- [ ] Itemized provider bills (not the summary statement — the itemized
      one, which the provider's billing office can send)
- [ ] Your plan's summary of benefits for the relevant service category
- [ ] Notes of every phone call: date, representative name, what was said

**For life claims:**

- [ ] Certified death certificates (several copies; agencies each keep
      their own)
- [ ] The policy's dec page or policy number
- [ ] Claim form from the insurer (they send it promptly once notified)
- [ ] Beneficiary ID as the insurer requests

## Walkthrough: auto claim (invented scenario)

A shopping-cart dents your parked car; no note left.

1. Photograph the dent and the lot. Check if the lot has cameras (ask the
   store manager; note the date/time).
2. Decide whether the repair cost is above your collision deductible
   (guide 02's small-loss line). *That decision is yours*; the arithmetic
   just informs it.
3. If filing: call the number on your dec page, give the facts, get the
   claim number.
4. The adjuster estimates the repair. You may choose a shop per your
   policy's terms; the conditions section explains your options.
5. Payment goes to you (or to you and the lender jointly if the car is
   financed — that's why the loss-payee line exists on the dec page).

## Walkthrough: homeowners claim (invented scenario)

A pipe bursts while you're at work; the kitchen ceiling is soaked.

1. Shut off the water. That's "mitigating further damage" — required by
   the conditions section, and it's also just correct.
2. Photos before cleanup: ceiling, floor, the pipe, the water line on the
   wall. Wide shot first, then details.
3. Call the claim number. Start the mitigation clock: a mitigation company
   can begin drying, and their invoice is part of the claim.
4. Start the inventory list (Worksheet D) room by room. Low-energy rule:
   one room per sitting is a complete unit of progress.
5. Keep every receipt: hotel if needed, fans, meals. Coverage D is
   receipt-driven.
6. Meet the adjuster with the binder: dec page, inventory, receipts,
   estimates.

## Walkthrough: health claim follow-up (invented scenario)

An EOB arrives showing a service as "not covered."

1. Read the reason code and its plain-language text on the EOB.
2. Check it against your plan documents (guide 07's skeleton): is this an
   excluded category, a prior-authorization miss, or a coding question?
3. Call the provider's billing office first for coding questions — many
   "denials" are billing artifacts that resubmit cleanly.
4. If it stands, guide 10 takes over: this is now a denial with a first
   response.

## Phone-call discipline

Every call: before dialing, have the claim number and your one-paragraph
description in front of you. During: write the date, the representative's
name, and what was promised. After: file the note in the binder. This is
the single highest-leverage habit in this entire manual.

## The calm rule for deadlines

Open your policy's conditions section once — today, while nothing is
wrong — and write the claim-reporting deadline on Worksheet A. Deadlines
found on a calm day are met on a bad day. That's the whole trick.
