# Glossary

> **Education only.** Plain-English definitions of paperwork vocabulary.
> These are study definitions, not legal definitions — your policy's own
> definitions section always controls.

## A

**Actual cash value (ACV)** — Payment based on what an item is worth used
(depreciated), rather than what a new one costs. Contrast: replacement
cost.

**Adjuster** — The insurer's investigator for a claim. Inspects damage,
requests documents, produces an estimate or decision.

**Allowed amount** — (Health) the contracted rate a plan recognizes for a
service. Cost-sharing is computed from this number, not from the
provider's sticker price.

**Appeal** — A formal request for an insurer to reconsider a decision.
Policies and plans state the deadlines and where to send appeals.

**Application** — What you told the insurer when you applied. Part of the
policy packet; worth an annual skim for stale statements.

**Assignment of benefits** — An arrangement letting a provider bill the
insurer directly. Read before signing; it changes who receives and
manages payments.

## B

**Beneficiary** — (Life) the person or entity designated to receive the
death benefit. The designation form controls, generally overriding wills.

**Binder (insurance)** — A short-term temporary coverage confirmation
used before the full policy is issued. (In this manual, "binder" also
means your paperwork folder — see guide 11.)

## C

**Cash value** — (Permanent life) the savings-like component that grows
per the policy's terms and can be borrowed against or surrendered.

**Claim** — A formal request for payment under a policy.

**Coinsurance** — (Health) your percentage share of the allowed amount
after the deductible; e.g. 20% coinsurance on a $1,000 allowed amount =
$200 from you.

**Collision coverage** — (Auto) pays for damage to your car from a
collision, regardless of fault, minus your deductible.

**Conditions** — The policy section with each side's duties and the
deadlines: reporting claims, proof of loss, cancellation.

**Comprehensive coverage** — (Auto) pays for damage to your car from
non-collision causes: theft, weather, fire, animals, glass.

**Copay** — (Health) a flat amount per service, independent of the
service's cost.

**Coverage limit** — The most a policy will pay for a given coverage.
Shown on the dec page.

## D

**Deductible** — The portion of a covered loss you pay before insurance
contributes. Per claim in property insurance; per year in most health
plans.

**Declarations page (dec page)** — The summary page listing insureds,
coverages, limits, deductibles, premium, and policy period.

**Depreciation** — The reduction in value from age and wear; the
mechanism behind ACV payments.

**Duties after loss** — The conditions-section list of what you must do
when a loss happens: report promptly, prevent further damage, cooperate.

## E

**Endorsement (rider)** — A form attached to a policy that adds, removes,
or changes coverage. Endorsements generally override the base text.

**EOB (Explanation of Benefits)** — (Health) a per-claim report of
billed, allowed, paid, and patient-owed amounts. Not a bill.

**Evidence of Coverage (EOC)** — (Medicare Advantage and similar) the
name for the full plan contract.

**Exclusion** — A stated cause of loss, item, or situation the policy
does not cover.

**Extended replacement cost** — A dwelling provision adding headroom
above the Coverage A limit (e.g. +25%) for rebuild-cost spikes.

## F

**Face amount** — (Life) the death benefit stated on the dec page.

## G

**Grace period** — The stated window after a missed premium during which
coverage continues.

## I

**Incontestability clause** — (Life) a provision limiting the insurer's
ability to contest application statements after the policy has been in
force for a stated period (commonly two years).

**Insuring agreement** — The policy's core promise, in one or two
sentences, modified by everything else in the document.

## L

**Loss of use (ALE)** — Coverage for extra living costs when a home is
unlivable (Coverage D in property forms). Receipt-driven.

**Loss payee / mortgagee** — A lender with an financial interest in the
insured property, named on the dec page; claim payments may include them.

## M

**Medical payments coverage** — Small no-fault coverage for others'
minor medical bills (Coverage F in property forms; MedPay in auto).

**Medical necessity** — (Health) a defined standard for whether a
service is covered; the concept most often cited in health denials.

## N

**Named perils** — A coverage structure where only listed causes of loss
are covered. Contrast: open perils.

**Network** — (Health) the set of providers with contracted rates.
In-network vs. out-of-network cost sharing differs accordingly.

**Non-renewal** — The insurer (or you) choosing not to continue the
policy at the end of its period, with notice rules in the conditions.

## O

**Open perils** — A coverage structure where everything is covered
except stated exclusions.

**Out-of-pocket maximum** — (Health) the yearly ceiling on your share of
allowed costs; after it, the plan pays covered in-network costs for the
rest of the year. Premiums don't count toward it.

## P

**Per stirpes / per capita** — (Life) two rules for what happens when a
named beneficiary has died; the policy defines which applies.

**Policy period** — The dates a policy is in force.

**Premium** — What you pay to keep the policy in force.

**Prior authorization** — (Health) pre-approval required for certain
services before they're performed.

**Proof of loss** — The formal documented statement of a claim some
policies require, with a deadline in the conditions.

## R

**Replacement cost value (RCV)** — Payment based on the cost of a new
equivalent, typically after you replace the item and submit receipts.

**Rider** — See endorsement.

## S

**Scheduled personal property** — An endorsement listing specific
valuable items individually, above category caps.

**Settlement options** — (Life) the ways a death benefit can be paid:
lump sum by default, or other arrangements described in the policy.

**Subrogation** — The insurer's right, after paying your claim, to
pursue recovery from the party at fault.

**Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)** — (Health) the standardized
plain-language summary every plan must provide.

## T

**Term life** — Life insurance covering a stated period, with no cash
value.

## U

**Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** — (Auto) coverage paying
you when an at-fault driver has no insurance or too little.
