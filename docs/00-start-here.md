# Start Here

> **Education only.** This manual teaches you how to *read* insurance
> paperwork. It is never coverage, legal, medical, or tax advice, and it never
> tells you what to buy. When a decision matters, involve your state insurance
> department, a licensed agent or broker, or an attorney.

## Who this is for

Anyone who has ever opened an insurance policy, hit a wall of defined terms,
and closed it again. Maybe you just rented your first apartment and the
renters policy is four pages of italics. Maybe a denial letter arrived and
you don't know what "not a covered benefit" is answering. This manual is for
that moment.

## The calm-voice promise

1. **Short sentences.** If a sentence needs a deep breath, it gets split.
2. **Round numbers.** Every example uses $100, $1,000, $10,000 — numbers you
   can check in your head.
3. **No scolding.** If you haven't read your policy in years, welcome. That's
   normal. That's who this is for.
4. **Small steps.** Every checklist is broken into pieces small enough to
   finish on a low-energy day.
5. **No urgency theater.** Nothing here is a countdown. Paperwork keeps.
   Except actual claim deadlines — those we flag honestly, and only those.

## How the manual is organized

- **Part 1 — Reading skills** (guides 01–03): how any policy is built, how
  the money math works, and how to decode a declarations page. Read these
  once and the rest gets easier.
- **Part 2 — Policy types** (guides 04–08): auto, renters, homeowners,
  health, and life, each translated section by section.
- **Part 3 — Doing things** (guides 09–11): filing a claim, answering a
  denial letter, and the annual review ritual.
- **Reference** (guide 12 + worksheets): the glossary and the policy-binder
  worksheets.

## A 10-minute first pass

If you read nothing else, do this:

1. Find your most recent declarations page (the summary sheet that came with
   your policy — usually page 1 or 2). Guide 03 decodes it.
2. Write down three numbers from it: your **premium**, your **deductible**,
   and your **coverage limit**. Guide 02 explains what each one actually does.
3. Put the policy, the declarations page, and your note in one folder. That
   folder is the start of your policy binder. The worksheets in
   `docs/worksheets/` give it a spine.

That's it. You've done more than most people ever do, and you did it in ten
minutes.

## Companion project

For Medicare-specific reading — Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage,
Summary Notices, the Annual Notice of Change — see the companion project
*medicare-decoded-guide*. This manual covers health-insurance paperwork in
general and stops where Medicare specifics begin.

## What this manual will never do

- Recommend a policy, carrier, or coverage amount.
- Interpret what *your* specific policy means for *your* specific situation.
- Draft appeal arguments for you (it shows the structure; the words are
  yours).
- Handle medical, legal, or tax questions.

What it *will* do: make the next envelope from your insurer feel like
paperwork instead of a threat.
