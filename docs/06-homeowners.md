# Homeowners Policies, Translated

> **Education only.** Your policy's wording controls everything. This guide
> explains what homeowners-policy sections *are*, not what you should buy.

The standard homeowners policy (you'll see form names like HO-3, HO-5, HO-6)
is organized as: property coverages A–D, liability coverages E–F, then
perils, exclusions, conditions, endorsements. Here's the map.

## The coverage letters

### Coverage A — Dwelling

The structure itself: walls, roof, attached garage, built-in appliances.
The limit here anchors everything else (B, C, D are often percentages of
A). Two reading skills:

- **Replacement cost vs. actual cash value** applies to the dwelling too.
  RCV rebuilds; ACV pays depreciated value. The dec page states which.
- **Extended/inflation endorsements** (e.g. "25% extended replacement")
  add headroom above the limit for rebuild-cost spikes. If your dec page
  shows one, note it.

### Coverage B — Other structures

Detached garage, fence, shed, driveway. Usually a flat percent of A
(commonly 10%). Read it as "10% of whatever A is," not a fixed number.

### Coverage C — Personal property

Your belongings, same mechanics as renters (guide 05): ACV vs. RCV, and
**special limits** on categories — jewelry, silverware, firearms, cash,
business property, electronics. The caps are the section to actually read
closely, because they're the ones people bump into.

### Coverage D — Loss of use (ALE)

Additional living expenses if the home is unlivable: hotel, food above
normal, pet boarding, laundry. Often 20% of A. Receipt-driven; keep
everything.

### Coverage E — Personal liability

Injury or property damage you're legally responsible for, plus the duty to
defend. Separate from property coverage entirely.

### Coverage F — Medical payments to others

Small no-fault coverage for guests' minor medical bills.

## Named perils vs. open perils

The biggest structural question in the policy, stated right in the form:

- **Named perils** (typical for B and C): only the *listed* causes of loss
  are covered — fire, lightning, windstorm, hail, explosion, riot, aircraft,
  vehicles, smoke, vandalism, theft, falling objects, weight of ice/snow,
  accidental discharge of water, and so on. If it's not listed, it's not
  covered.
- **Open perils** (typical for A under HO-3 and HO-5): everything is
  covered *except* what's excluded. The exclusions section becomes the
  real definition of coverage.

Same event, different answers: mold from a slow leak is usually excluded
under either structure (long-term seepage exclusion), while sudden burst-pipe
water damage is a named peril and covered under both. The *named perils
list* and the *exclusions list* are two halves of one boundary — read them
as a pair.

## The famous exclusions, plainly

- **Flood** (defined as surface water) — excluded; separate flood policies
  exist through the National Flood Insurance Program and private carriers.
- **Earthquake** — excluded; separate coverage or endorsement.
- **Sewer/drain backup** — often excluded in the base form but available
  as an endorsement; check both the exclusion list *and* the endorsement
  list, since endorsements can add it back.
- **Wear and tear, rot, mold from neglect** — excluded; insurance is for
  sudden and accidental, not maintenance.
- **Business activity** — small caps or exclusion; a dedicated endorsement
  exists for home businesses.

## Wind/hail and percentage deductibles

Many policies carry a separate percentage deductible for wind/hail or
hurricane losses (guide 02's wrinkle). Example: 2% of a $300,000 Coverage A
limit = $6,000, applied only to wind/hail claims, while the standard
$1,000 deductible applies to everything else. The dec page shows both rows.

## A claim-shaped mental model

- Kitchen fire damages cabinets → Coverage A (built-ins) + C (loose items),
  standard deductible.
- Wind rips shingles off → A, wind/hail deductible (check which one your
  dec page assigns).
- Sump pump overflows the basement → only if you have the water-backup
  endorsement; otherwise excluded.
- Tree falls on the detached garage → B.
- Family of four in a hotel for six weeks after the fire → D, receipts.

## The annual homeowners check

During the guide-11 ritual: has Coverage A drifted far from local rebuild
costs (you can't know precisely, but a limit untouched for a decade is a
flag), did you add anything above a special-limit cap, is the mortgagee
line current, and do you know where the wind/hail deductible percentage
sits?
